# Prose rules

Applies to prose only: blog posts, docs, PR text, messages, social copy. Never touch code, config, or technical terms. Review every prose output against this before delivering.

## The rules

The last one is the escape hatch.

1. Never use a metaphor, simile or other figure of speech which you are used to seeing in print.
2. Never use a long word where a short one will do.
3. If it is possible to cut a word out, always cut it out.
4. Never use the passive where you can use the active.
5. Never use a foreign phrase, a scientific word or a jargon word if you can think of an everyday English equivalent.
6. Never hedge. Cut "arguably", "somewhat", "virtually"; state the claim.
7. Never turn a verb into a noun. A named actor and a strong verb: "decide", not "reach a decision".
8. Put the emphatic word last in the sentence.
9. Break any of these rules sooner than say anything outright barbarous. Keep a vivid figure or a technical term when the plain replacement would be worse or less accurate.

## Voice

- Casual, everyday human language. Fewer syllables, simpler words.
- Not marketing grandiose ("revolutionize", "transform", "unleash", "empower").
- Not white paper ("leverage", "utilize", "facilitate", "enable").
- Not consultant-speak ("drive alignment", "unlock value", "best-in-class").

## Specificity

- First-order outcomes, not abstract benefits.
- Name concrete things, not categories. If you can't picture it, it's too vague.
- No dangling abstractions ("tell the story in the right way": what story? what way?).
- No lists of three abstract nouns. No outcomes you can't directly deliver (revenue, conversion, win rate).

## Structure

- Progressive disclosure: one idea, then the next. Don't stack five concepts in a sentence.
- Lead with what matters most.
- Show, don't tell: illustrate with examples, not definitions.
- Minimum viable context: if a sentence can go without losing the narrative, cut it. Don't explain *why* when *that* is enough. Trust the reader.

## Style

For any prose — posts, case studies, docs:

- Three or more sentences per paragraph. Two reads as bullet points.
- One job per sentence. State it. Don't explain why unless the why is the point.
- No colons in running prose, except the "X is simple: …" construction. No dashes doing a colon's job. Join with "since", "so", "which", or a full stop.
- Oxford comma.
- The problem never contains the solution. If a sentence is only true because of what was built, it belongs later.
- Stay at the level of the section. An intro is about the problem, not the format, the tools, or the mechanism.
- Headings name the activity or the subject. Never first person, never the tool.
- When a sentence is dictated, place it. Don't rewrite the paragraph around it.
- Published pieces are the style guide. Match their paragraph jobs and sentence rhythm, not just their vocabulary.

## Test

Does this sound like a real person explaining something to another human, or like marketing copy?
